Ambiguity-Aware Multimodal Editing for Code-Based Visualization

Anonymous Authors

Submitted to PacificVis 2027 · Conference Track

Abstract

With the growing popularity of vibe coding, users increasingly use large language models (LLMs) to edit visualizations. However, users’ editing requests are often brief and underspecified, which can result in ambiguity and lead LLMs to make unintended changes. Although existing systems offer different ways, including sketching, direct manipulation and dynamic widgets, to help users express visualization editing requests, they provide limited support for diagnosing the ambiguities and adaptively offering appropriate clarification mechanisms. It shifts to users the burden of recognizing what is missing and determining how to express it. To address this gap, we present ACME, a visualization editing tool that provides Ambiguity-aware guidance to help users refine Code-based visualizations through Multimodal Editing. Guided by this ambiguity-aware editing framework, ACME uses LLMs to represent requests as editing actions and identify unresolved operations, target objects, or parameters. It then selects and dynamically configures or generates natural language questions, inline widgets, or direct manipulation interactions through which users clarify their intent and refine the visualization. We conduct a user study that examines how the framework affects editing performance and users’ experiences of expressing and clarifying their intent.
